The artist

A creative journey

Oaheng Motsitsi a multimedia artist whose work is a reflection of A follower of Christ Jesus and explores genre.

About

from traditional media and love for charcoal, ther works centred around being a voice to the voiceless. Genre centred work has inherently influenced the spiritual background of said works and experiences. Moving from traditional media to twine work this method of expression has become a key element in the exploration process. The twine creates a bridge between reality and spiritual (Christianity) truths .

Artist biography

Born in Upington in 2001 to a family that never stopped moving, she grew up across South Africa before her teenage years settled in Pretoria. Somewhere between those places she found that a pencil could hold what words could not, and by fourteen, the decision was already made.

She works first in charcoal and pastel, raw, uncut, unafraid of feeling, before pulling the drawn line into photography, paint, glass and woven material. Her subjects are everyday: a woman at a basin, a figure in the rubble, a salon on a street the city forgets.

Every piece is an invitation to look closer. The viewer becomes an anonymous collaborator, asked to sit with a life that is not their own, and to find, in the overlooked, something worth keeping.

“My work lives between reality and spiritual identity”

Her practice is still becoming, moving between charcoal and twine weaving, the sacred and the ordinary, the known and the next.

Artist statement
“I reframe identity through local voices.”

Just like Oaheng — ah aha, bonono ba hae.

Read the full statement

I am interested in the discourse of the “African art narrative” as well as honouring God in my work. My works challenge the African narrative; I believe that as a continent we share similarities, but I aim to highlight the differences, as they are just as important in forming the identity of the Voiceless in our societies — in my case, South Africans and those dwelling within the borders of our country.

I reframe identity through local voices. I practise in various mediums, which allows me to experiment with the emotions conjured by the viewer when relating to a topic. In the representation of God in my work, my aim is to exercise the gift given to the first Creative — culture-creator — which was the Spirit of God. In remembrance, I create works that honour our Lord through a contemporary lens.

Working with people has shifted how I approach the process of art-making. As a multidisciplinary artist, I have found that the most authentic way to go about it should be raw and uncut — and some of my favourite mediums to achieve this are charcoal and pastel, to name a few.
