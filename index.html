I
Origins

A childhood always on the move

Born in Upington, 2001, into a family that never stopped moving — the Northern Cape to Pretoria, place to place. Somewhere in the in-between, a child discovered that a pencil could hold what words could not. By fourteen, the decision was made.

“at 14, I chose
this.”↘
II
The room for unsaid things

Raw & Uncut

Charcoal and pastel became her first language — a room where unsaid emotion could be spoken without confrontation, without limit. Nothing polished. Everything felt.

“I find the most raw and uncut emotion through charcoal and pastel.”

— Oaheng Motsitsi
III
Woman of Africa

Mosali wa Afrika

She turns the camera and the cloth on the street itself — placing the body in the dust, the salon, the rubble. The viewer becomes an anonymous collaborator in lives the world is taught to overlook.

IV
Past, Present & Future · Weedlovers Market, 2025

Reframing the narrative

Her paint turns to the nation. Orania, the flag, the forbidden fruit — works that argue with history and refuse a single, flattened “African narrative.” The differences, she insists, are the point. Shown at the Past, Present & Future First Thursday exhibition, Weedlovers Market.

V
Material & faith · ContraJHB

Made by hand

Twine, resin, glass and found material pull the drawn into the physical. In the making, she honours the first Creative — the Spirit of God — through a contemporary lens.

VI
Collection · The Viewing Room Gallery, 2025

Monna ke Nku

Monna ke nku — a man is a sheep. The body of work she showed at The Viewing Room Gallery: altars, birds and small sacrificial figures on grounds of red and earth, where her faith meets her fascination with the Voiceless — the silent endurance of those the world overlooks.

VII
The archive

Becoming

Butterflies, blooms and bright studies trace an artist still in motion — living between the drawn line and the made object, the known and the next.

Artist Statement
“I reframe identity through local voices.”

Just like Oaheng — ah aha, bonono ba hae.

Read the full statement

I am interested in the discourse of the “African art narrative” as well as honouring God in my work. My works challenge the African narrative; I believe that as a continent we share similarities, but I aim to highlight the differences, as they are just as important in forming the identity of the Voiceless in our societies — in my case, South Africans and those dwelling within the borders of our country.

I reframe identity through local voices. I practise in various mediums, which allows me to experiment with the emotions conjured by the viewer when relating to a topic, and to juxtapose the subject against the reaction. In the representation of God in my work, my aim is to exercise the gift given to the first Creative — culture-creator — which was the Spirit of God. In remembrance, I create works that honour our Lord through a contemporary lens.

Working with people has shifted how I approach the process of art-making. As a multidisciplinary artist, I have found that the most authentic way to go about it should be raw and uncut — and some of my personal favourite mediums to achieve this are charcoal and pastel, to name a few.
